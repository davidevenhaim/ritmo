Product plan & build report

Your training, your people, your app.

SoGym opens on you: today's steps against your goal, your streak, what you have planned, and a line to an AI trainer that knows your body and your gear. Friends, routines, quests and step leagues are one tap away behind the + button, never in the way. Free with no limits for individuals; studios, clubs and trainers pay for the tools they use to run groups.

The pitch

The problem. Fitness apps are lonely spreadsheets. Strava solved social for runners and cyclists and became a 120M-user business; nobody has done it for the gym, mobility, and everyday movement together. Hevy and Strong log sets. Fitbod generates plans. Instagram has the hot workout videos but you cannot do anything with them. The social layer and the useful layer are in different apps.

The insight. A workout post is only valuable if it is executable. In SoGym every post that contains a routine has a Try it button that copies it into your library and starts a set-by-set player. Recommendations are not likes, they are people saying "I did this, do it too". That makes the feed a distribution channel for routines, and routines are the retention engine.

The wedge. Steps. Everyone with a phone already generates them, health apps already store them, and sharing a daily number is the lowest-friction social action in fitness. Steps get people posting on day one, before they have a routine to share.

One-liner for the store: "Your training, your streak, your people. Free, with an AI trainer that actually knows you."

Decisions worth knowing

  • Client-generated UUIDs. Routines and posts get a v4 id on the device, so the same row exists locally and hosted and a sign-in never re-keys content. Legacy local ids are migrated on first pull.
  • Counters are never written by clients. Insert policies require zero counts; triggers maintain like_count, comment_count, try_count and follower counts.
  • Quota lives in SQL. consume_coach_message increments atomically, rolls back when blocked, and only the service role can execute it. Free messages per week is a function secret so it can change without a deploy.
  • Prompt and tools are shared verbatim between the on-device coach and the edge function, so one eval set covers both.
  • Handle collisions are resolved server-side; if the local handle is taken the app keeps the server's choice rather than failing sign-in.

Not done in this pass: the project itself is not provisioned (needs a Supabase account and Apple/Google OAuth credentials), and the Firebase client for push tokens is not wired into the Flutter app. Both are listed under Run it.

v0.3: video and creators

The third pass, again straight from the roadmap. Bytes never touch our servers: a video edge function hands the phone a one-time direct-upload ticket from Cloudflare Stream (default) or Mux, the provider transcodes, and its signed webhook flips the row to ready. The feed card shows processing, ready and failed states by itself. Uploads are gated to approved creators, every ready clip is screened by Claude before anyone else sees it, and anything a member reports lands in a moderation queue.

Decisions worth knowing

  • Direct upload, not proxied. The phone talks to the video host with a single-use ticket; the function never sees the bytes, so a 200 MB clip costs us one small request.
  • Server columns are protected by a trusted-write flag. Clients may edit post text; a before-update trigger resets counters and moderation columns unless the writer is a moderator or a trusted SQL function that raised a transaction-local flag. The v0.2 like counter was re-issued to use it. Verified on a local Postgres: a plain user's like still counts, their attempt to un-hide a post is silently reverted.
  • Reports are polymorphic (post, comment, profile) with a partial unique index, so the automated screener can file without a reporter and members cannot pile on twice.
  • Screening is advisory. Claude never deletes; it hides and files a report. Bare torsos in a gym context are explicitly fine, and ambiguous stills are marked safe so the queue does not fill with false flags.
  • The demo walks the whole flow. Without a backend the uploader is a stand-in that turns a picked clip into a placeholder video that becomes ready after six seconds; applying as a creator is instant; everyone is a moderator; two reports are seeded so the queue is not empty.

Not done in this pass: no Cloudflare or Mux account is wired (keys go in as function secrets), the webhook URL must be registered with the provider once, and upload progress is coarse because the Dart http client does not stream request bodies. Also outstanding from v0.2: project provisioning, OAuth credentials, Firebase client.

Decisions worth knowing

  • One table feeds everything. Leagues, streaks and badges all read step_days; there is no separate leaderboard state to drift. Standings are computed on read, results are only materialised at the weekly close.
  • Today never breaks a streak. Both the SQL and Dart versions skip an in-progress day, so someone at 4,000 steps at lunch still sees their streak.
  • Privacy follows the league. Daily counts are only readable by people who share a league with you; outside a league a step post is still the only way your number leaves the device.
  • Badges are diffed, never re-awarded. The award function computes what you qualify for and inserts what is new, so it is safe to call after every sync.
  • Demo parity. Two seeded leagues (one you are in, one to join with code IRON77), seeded members with deterministic daily steps, and the same badge rules in Dart, so the whole loop can be walked without a backend.

Not done in this pass: true background sync while the app is closed (WorkManager on Android, BGTaskScheduler on iOS) and the watch complications, both native targets that cannot be built or tested on this machine today. The sync entry point and the rank API they need are in place.

Decisions worth knowing

  • The phone owns the plan. Schedule and quests are stored on-device. Nothing about what a person intends to do leaves the phone unless they post it. Hosted sync of the schedule is a later, opt-in step.
  • Free means free. No message caps, no locked screens for individuals. Revenue comes from group tools for studios, clubs and trainers (see Monetization), so the product never has a reason to nag a person who just wants to walk more.
  • Nearby without location. The app does not ask for GPS. Hosted "nearby" ranks public leagues by members and recency and shows the owner's area label; a coarse-location opt-in can refine that later without a schema change.
  • Photos stay small and local. 400px JPEG at quality 78 is about 20 to 40 KB, fine for prefs and for web localStorage. The hosted profile keeps the emoji until an avatar bucket exists.
  • Everything on the page is mine. The sections read as my plan, my people, my week, my training. Other people appear as a strip of friends and a shared quest bar, never as a ranked list I have to beat.
  • CoG is cooperative by design. One friend, one bar, summed. It is the anti-leaderboard for people who bounce off competition.

Decisions worth knowing

  • The goal is the only question. Nobody should need to know what a hypertrophy rep range is to build a routine. Picking "Build muscle" sets 4×10 with 90 seconds rest; "Get stronger" sets 5×5 with 150; yoga and flexibility switch to holds. The user can still edit everything afterwards.
  • The catalogue is generated, not committed by hand. RepDB's licence forbids redistributing its rows as a dataset, so the merge lives in a script. If the repo ever goes public, gitignore the JSON and run the script at build time. That decision is written down, not assumed.
  • One muscle vocabulary. RepDB names muscles anatomically (rectus_abdominis, latissimus_dorsi); the app speaks free-exercise-db (abdominals, lats). The build script normalises on the way in, so a body-part filter works across all three sources instead of silently missing 500 exercises.
  • Sixty kit names, ten chips. The merged catalogue names about sixty pieces of equipment. They collapse into ten groups (No kit, Dumbbell, Barbell, Machine, Cardio kit…) so the filter row stays one line, and every chip offered is one that actually has exercises behind it for that goal.
  • Breathing is hand-authored on purpose. No open breathwork dataset exists anywhere. The patterns are written from public technique descriptions, which are facts rather than anyone's copyright, and they carry exactly the inhale/hold/exhale/hold/cycles fields the UI needs.

Not done in this pass: 115 of the merged exercises have no image (mostly RepDB entries whose illustrations are premium), the yoga poses have no muscle tags so they only answer to the Yoga and Flexibility goals rather than to a body part, and the routine a goal creates is a single day — multi-day splits still come from the editor or the AI trainer.

Decisions worth knowing

  • Two metrics were substituted, not faked. The handoff's stat row is Volume / Steps / Resting HR. Steps are real. We do not record load per set, and reading heart rate would widen the Health permission past the steps-only promise the app makes in Settings, so the ring shows the week against the user's own session target and the third card shows the sets behind it. Same shapes, numbers the app can stand behind. The follow feed's Volume / Time / PR strip became the three counts each post actually carries, with the standout one in accent where PR sat.
  • Phosphor's Flutter package does not build on Flutter 3.47 — PhosphorIconData extends IconData, and IconData is now final. The font ships inside that package under MIT, so it is vendored directly and the glyphs the design uses are declared as codepoints in Nx. Tree-shaking cuts the shipped font to 8 KB.
  • Inter needs its weight axis set. It is one variable file, so FontWeight.w500 alone renders at 400; every token style carries a matching fontVariations entry.
  • Navigation stayed ours, until v0.8. The handoff's bar is Me / Start / Community with a raised middle button; v0.7 kept three tabs — Me / Friends / Build — and took only the handoff's styling: 21px icon over a 9.5px label, active #e9e9ed, inactive #75798c, hairline top border. v0.8 adopted the raised middle button after all; see below.

Build: the drag timeline

Step 1 is the handoff's six themes, each one a real filter over the catalogue so the count on a card is the number of exercises actually behind it: Bodyweight 186, Gym 742, Flexibility 244, Handstand 51, Calisthenics 79, Core 204. The theme also carries how its work is done, so a session built from it arrives with sets, reps and rest already set rather than blank.

Step 2 is the timeline. The card is the drop target and its ring goes accent while a drag is over it. Library tiles are both draggable and tappable. Each exercise contributes a segment whose width is its share of the session, estimated as sets times rest plus about forty seconds of work per set. Rows carry an index, the exercise media, a remove control and a drag handle; dragging one over another splices it to that position and the moving row's ring goes accent. Step 3 names it, shows theme, exercise count and duration, and offers two toggles before saving.

Saving is real: the routine goes into the library, onto Saturday in the plan, and — when the share toggle is on — out as a routine post the user's followers can copy. The invite toggle starts a shared quest with a followed friend.

The exercise media placeholder is filled in. The handoff's striped grey tiles are where looping media goes; this app has images for most of the catalogue, so tiles show the real exercise and fall back to the striped placeholder only where an image is missing.

Decisions worth knowing

  • The Start sheet became the + menu, and in v0.8 the raised middle button the handoff drew it from. Its five sections are intact and in order: resume, your routines, recommended, shared by friends, create new. The old four-option menu is gone.
  • The staggered row entry was dropped, the ring feedback kept. Driving a 60ms-per-index entry animation from inside a drag target left rows stuck part-way through their fade. The interaction the design depends on is the accent ring on the drop target and on the row being moved, and that is intact.
  • The reorder is hand-rolled, not a ReorderableListView. Nesting a second scrollable inside the page's list left every row past the first unpainted. A plain column of drag targets also matches the handoff's own semantics more directly.
  • The AI draft is grounded, not decorative. It picks from the theme's own library against the athlete's equipment, spreads the block across muscle groups so it is not five variations of one movement, and answers the selected prompt: a short session returns four exercises, "go heavy" returns compounds only, "easy on my joints" returns bodyweight and stretching only. Swapping in the hosted coach is a change to one function.
  • Legacy UI is deleted, not left dormant. The goal-first Build screen, the routines list, the routine editor, the routine detail page and the old profile page are gone, along with the training-goal, body-part and equipment-group models that only served them.

Next in this pass: the handoff's Community, AI coach, exercise library and coach dashboard screens are not built, and the in-session player — the one flow the prototype stubs to a toast — is being designed. Friends moved to Nocturne in v0.8.

v0.9: a session picked for you, and the Community tab

Two halves of the same idea. The Start button stops being a menu and starts being an answer — here is your session, here is why — and the tab it displaced becomes the place the studios and coaches live, which is where the money is.

Picked for you

The first thing under Start is now a session assembled for the athlete in front of it. Nothing about it is generated prose: every exercise comes out of the 1,443-exercise catalogue through four hard filters, and the chips on the card name them, because a recommendation nobody can interrogate is a horoscope.

What the profile saysWhat it changes
EquipmentA hard filter. A dumbbell athlete is never offered a barbell lift; the chip names the kit the picks actually use.
ExperienceNew in this pass: ExperienceLevel on the profile, asked in onboarding. The catalogue grades every exercise beginner / intermediate / expert and the pick respects it. A beginner also gets one exercise and one set fewer.

The last 48 hoursMuscles trained in the logged sessions are skipped while the pool has anything else — resolved through the routine that was run, and through the day of it the log names, so finishing push day does not retire the whole split.

Picks are spread deliberately: one exercise per muscle group before any group gets a second, and two exercises sharing their first two words count as the same movement, so a session never comes back as five variations of a row. Start runs it in the player without saving it; Save keeps it in the library; the circular arrow rotates to the next theme the goal allows. Building a routine by hand is still the Build screen — now opened from Start rather than from a tab.

The catalogue, graded

The library grew to 1,443 and, more usefully, every exercise now carries one of three grades — beginner, intermediate, advanced. The open sources disagreed with each other (free-exercise-db says "expert", RepDB has its own scale, the yoga poses were ungraded, and 47 rows carried no grade at all), so the vocabulary is normalised on the way in and a row without a grade is not allowed into the asset. Thirty-six exercises were written in-house to fill the ends of the ladder the open sources leave thin: supported sit-to-stands, kneeling planks, wall hinges and heel slides for someone starting out, long-lever and single-leg work for someone who is not.

The grade is a first-class filter, not a label. The builder's library splits into three shelves with a chip and a count each, and the athlete's own level carries an accent dot; the exercise library gained a Level row next to muscle, equipment and type; and the Start recommender was already filtering on it — it just could not see the "advanced" rows until the vocabularies matched.

Curation is now data. The hand-graded levels and the editorial rows exist in no upstream source, so tool/build_catalogue.py treats assets/data/exercises.json as authoritative: a rerun keeps every row and field already there and only appends what the sources have added. --fresh throws that away deliberately.

v0.10: look before you accept, and the same session at your level

Somebody else's routine used to arrive as a name and a count. "Copy to mine" was one tap away and "Look inside" opened the player — so the only way to find out what a coach's programme actually asked of you was to start doing it. Two things changed: every shared routine now opens on a preview, and the preview can rebuild the session at another grade.

The preview

Everything arriving from someone else — a friend's share, a coach's programme, a post in the feed, an invite to train together — lands on /routine/:id first. It reads the routine against the 1,443-exercise catalogue and shows what the athlete is being asked to agree to.

On the pageWhat it answers
Author, description, tags, and a kicker that says whether this is a coach programme, a friend's share or your own routineWho is asking, and in what capacity.
Days · exercises · minutes · level, then a bar showing how the exercises split across the three gradesHow big it is and how hard, from the exercises themselves rather than from the title.
"A step above your level — you train beginner"Where it sits relative to this athlete, in one line.
"Needs barbell · cable · kettlebells", and underneath, in accent, the ones they have not said they ownWhether they can actually do it on Tuesday.

Start now and Accept pinned to the bottomAccept clones it into the library and credits the author; the button then reads "In your routines".

The same session, at another level

Underneath sits the answer to "this looks too hard": three chips — Beginner, Intermediate, Advanced — with the routine's own grade marked as shared and the athlete's marked your level. Picking a different one re-levels the session on the spot. When the routine and the athlete disagree, the app offers first, without being asked: "Written for intermediate athletes. Tap to see the same session with beginner movements."

The rule it follows is that the shape of the session belongs to its author. Same days, same order, same muscle in the same slot; only the movement in each slot changes, to one the catalogue grades at the level asked for. A swap must share a primary muscle, may never cross out of its discipline (a stretch is swapped for a stretch, never a deadlift), is scored on matching force direction, mechanic, category and equipment — kit the athlete owns scores up, kit they do not scores down — and no movement already in the routine can be swapped in twice. Sets and rest then carry the difficulty the movement cannot: a step down is one set fewer and fifteen seconds more rest, a step up the reverse. Reps are left alone, because they are the author's prescription and rewriting them turns "the same programme, easier" into a different programme.

Where the library has nothing at that grade — nothing in the catalogue is graded advanced for the forearms, so a farmer's walk has nowhere to go — the original stays and the row says so. The summary counts it: "4 exercises swapped for beginner ones, 1 kept as it is." Accepting an adapted version saves it under its own name with the author still credited.

Decisions worth knowing

  • The leveller is a pure function (lib/core/routine_insight.dart), like the recommender, and is tested against the real catalogue: every swap is graded at the target, keeps the muscle, stays inside its discipline, never repeats, and levels the same way twice.
  • Reading a routine is separate from re-levelling it. RoutineInsight answers "what is this?" and is used by the preview card, the day timings and the verdict; RoutineLeveller answers "what would this be at another grade?".
  • Tapping an invite no longer starts a workout. It opens the preview at the day you were invited to. A booked class has no routine behind it, so it goes back to Community instead.
  • A long label on a block button now ellipsises rather than overflowing its row — found by the preview's "Accept beginner" button in a widget test at phone width.

Why Flutter, not Kotlin

The brief allowed either. The deciding factors were speed to two stores and the health integration.

Chosen

Flutter + Dart

One codebase ships iOS, Android and a web demo. The health plugin wraps HealthKit and Health Connect behind one API. Riverpod for state, go_router for navigation, and hot reload makes the design iteration loop minutes, not builds.

Rejected for now

Kotlin (Android) or KMP

Native Kotlin means a second Swift codebase for iOS before launch, or Kotlin Multiplatform with Compose Multiplatform for iOS which is still maturing for camera, video and health APIs. Revisit if we need deep Wear OS or Android-first hardware integrations.

Trade-off accepted: Flutter's web build is a demo surface, not the product. Video playback and health data are mobile-only in production.

Architecture

The MVP runs entirely on-device with a repository layer written so that a hosted backend swaps in without touching the UI. Every screen reads from Riverpod providers; providers talk to repositories; repositories persist to local JSON today and to Supabase tomorrow.

AI coach design

The coach is an agent with a small tool surface, not a prompt that dumps 1,443 exercises into context. That keeps each request cheap and every recommendation grounded in an exercise the app can actually render and play.

sequenceDiagram
  participant U as Athlete
  participant A as App
  participant C as Claude (claude-opus-5)
  U->>A: "Build me a plan"
  A->>C: system (persona, rules) + profile + tools
  C->>A: tool_use search_exercises {muscle: chest, equipment: dumbbell}
  A->>C: tool_result 12 ids filtered to the athlete's gear
  C->>A: tool_use search_exercises {muscle: back …}
  A->>C: tool_result …
  C->>A: tool_use save_plan {days: […], nutrition_notes, weekly_step_goal}
  A->>A: planToRoutine → Routine saved, unknown ids dropped
  A->>C: tool_result "Saved routine … 3 days"
  C->>A: text summary
  A->>U: bubble + plan card (Open · Share)

Design choices

  • Grounding by tool, not by context. search_exercises applies the athlete's equipment filter server-side of the model, so the model cannot pick a barbell move for someone with dumbbells. Unknown ids in save_plan are dropped defensively.
  • Strict schemas. Both tools use strict: true with additionalProperties: false, so the plan JSON always parses into a Routine. No regex over prose.
  • Profile in the system prompt, cached. The persona block carries a cache_control breakpoint; the profile follows it so cache hits survive across turns.
  • Effort tuned for chat. output_config.effort: medium keeps replies snappy; plan generation still runs three to five tool calls in one request.
  • Refusal handling. A stop_reason: refusal returns a friendly redirect instead of a crash.
  • Safety rules in the prompt. Pain, injury and eating-disorder cues get conservative advice and a referral. Diet restrictions are hard constraints.
  • Always demoable. With no key, LocalCoach generates a split by goal and days per week from the same catalogue and answers diet questions with the same restriction logic. Tests cover it.

Done in v0.2: the loop now runs in a Supabase edge function that holds the key, streams the reply, logs tool calls for evals, and enforces per-user quotas. Same tool definitions, same client UI. The on-device path with a user key remains for demos.

Next for the coach

  • Progression memory: the coach reads completed workouts and the last plan, then proposes weight and rep increases.
  • Weekly check-in: a scheduled run every Sunday that reads steps and workouts and posts a private recap with one adjustment.
  • Eval set: 40 profiles × 5 prompts scored on gear compliance, diet compliance, day count, and exercise validity. Gate every prompt change on it.

Trains 3 to 5 times a week, already screenshots Hevy or Strong to a group chat, follows fitness creators, wants friends to copy their split.

  3. Coach plan loop. "My AI coach built me this" posts show the plan and the athlete's goal. Readers with the same goal tap Build mine.

Monetization

Changed in v0.5. Individuals never pay and never hit a limit. The people who pay are the ones who run other people's training: studios, clubs, gyms and personal trainers, who get tools to manage groups on top of the same free app their members already use. An open-source core keeps the free promise credible and makes the app easy to trust with health data.

Risks and mitigations

RiskWhy it mattersMitigation
Empty-feed cold startSocial apps die without content on day oneSeeded community, creator imports before public beta, coach plans are shareable content from minute one
Health data privacy and store reviewHealthKit and Health Connect reviews are strictRead-only STEPS, nothing leaves the device until a share tap, privacy policy activity wired, no health-based ad targeting
Bad or unsafe AI adviceFitness and diet advice carries liabilityConservative prompt rules, restriction enforcement in code, disclaimers, eval suite, human review of flagged conversations
Model cost at scaleFree-tier chat could burn cashPrompt caching, medium effort, 5 free messages a week, tool grounding keeps context small
Video moderationUGC video invites abuseBuilt in v0.3: creators-only uploads, Claude screening of every clip, report flow, auto-hide at three reports, moderator queue with an action log
License compliancewger content is CC-BY-SAAttribution on every wger exercise, share-alike honoured for derived content, legal review before v1.0
